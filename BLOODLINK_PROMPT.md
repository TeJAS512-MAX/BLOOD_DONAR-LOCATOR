# BloodLink Website Generation Prompt

Create a complete, polished, responsive homepage prototype for **BloodLink**, a humanitarian blood donation network. The experience should feel like a future-facing digital product with a distinctive identity, not a generic healthcare template. Its message is: **Every drop connects a life.** The prototype connects voluntary donors, hospitals and authorised blood banks in moments when finding the right next step matters.

## Art Direction

- Build a cinematic, immersive first viewport around a custom 3D macro-world of a bloodstream: one beautiful, unmistakable red blood cell suspended in a deep burgundy-black environment, with smaller cells and fine living signal trails receding into the distance. No blood, injury, needles, people, text, logos or UI inside the artwork.
- Keep the hero visual full-bleed and edge-to-edge. Preserve generous dark negative space on the left for the brand and message; make the cell the visual anchor on the right. Blend the image with restrained dark gradients for readability, not with a floating text card.
- Use an intentional palette of obsidian, oxblood, garnet, warm ivory and luminous coral-red. The result should feel compassionate, scientifically wondrous, premium and clear, not horror-themed, sterile or overly cyberpunk.
- Use refined editorial typography: an expressive geometric display face for the BloodLink wordmark and headlines, an exceptionally readable sans-serif for product copy, and small monospaced labels for network metadata.
- Add a precise custom BloodLink droplet mark. Use thin signal lines, gentle atmospheric light and subtle 3D depth to create a memorable, ownable visual language.

## Hero Composition

- Place a restrained, transparent navigation over the hero with links to Find a Donor, Urgent Needs, Our Network and Donation Guide, plus a clear Become a Donor action.
- Make **BloodLink** the strongest brand signal in the first viewport. Pair it with the supporting line **Every drop connects a life.**
- Supporting copy: **Bringing voluntary donors, hospitals and authorised blood banks closer together - so help is easier to find when it matters.**
- Include two actions: **Find a donor** and **Become a donor**.
- Keep the first viewport focused on the brand, one message, one short explanation and the actions. Do not add hero statistics, schedules, event listings, detached badges, promo stickers or information cards.

## Homepage Sections

1. **Find a donor:** Create a simple, intuitive discovery form with blood group and location fields. Show a few illustrative example matches with clear location and availability labels. Make the form usable and filter the sample results when submitted. Clearly state that no live donor data or matching service is connected.
2. **Urgent requests:** Show a concise, legible feed of example blood requests, including blood group, hospital, location, units and priority. Give each example a hospital-verification state and an accessible request detail dialog. Explicitly label the records as demonstrations, not active medical requests. Let a visitor carry the request's blood group and area into the donor search.
3. **Blood bank network:** Provide a searchable example directory of hospital and blood-bank partners. Use clear location and partner-type labels. State that the directory is illustrative and not a live verified service list.
4. **Donation awareness:** Include a calm, accessible accordion covering eligibility, preparation, the appointment and donation intervals. Avoid giving personalised medical advice; direct visitors to local donation teams for current requirements.
5. **Our impact:** Tell a human-scale story about how separated blood components can support different types of patient care. Use a custom animated 3D orbit illustration to show one donation branching into red cells, plasma and platelets. Qualify any claim that one donation may help up to three people.
6. **Take action:** Finish with an unmistakable Become a Donor invitation. Open a short demo registration flow and explain that form data is not sent to a real service or saved.

## Interaction and Build Requirements

- Make the page work as a real responsive React experience, not a static mockup. Implement search filtering, directory filtering, accordion controls, navigation on small screens, request detail dialogs and donor registration success feedback.
- Clearly mark all example locations, requests, profiles and partner listings as fictional/demo content. Never imply that real donors, hospitals, blood banks, emergency services or medical integrations are available.
- Design for desktop and mobile, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, semantic form labels, accessible dialogs and reduced-motion preferences.
- Use a small number of deliberate motions: a slow atmospheric hero drift, subtle moving/pulsing connection signals, and a gently rotating component orbit. Motion should add life and hierarchy without distracting from reading or actions.
- Keep the visual system spacious and editorial. Avoid dashboard layouts, icon grids, statistic strips, decorative pill clusters and unnecessary cards. Use containers only for interaction areas where they improve clarity.
- Use high contrast, responsive spacing, polished hover states and accessible button targets. Do not use emoji.
- Deliver the homepage with complete copy, working prototype interactions and a clear concept-prototype safety note. The website should be ready to preview and deploy without needing any real medical integrations.
